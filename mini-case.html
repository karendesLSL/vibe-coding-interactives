Interactive Boardroom Case Study for Executive Leadership

Your Assigned Role: Lead Independent Director

To fully understand fiduciary principles, we must observe how they operate under fire. In this scenario, you are the Lead Independent Director of Zenith Technologies (NASDAQ: ZNTH), a high-growth autonomous systems manufacturer. You have been selected to protect shareholder interests during a turbulent sequence of corporate events.

Character Profile

Alex Mercer, MBA

15 years in enterprise governance. Known for independent ethics and deep legal literacy.

Active Persona
The Enterprise

Zenith Technologies

Publicly-traded. Incorporated in Delaware. Currently seeking a major market expansion through automated logistics acquisitions.

Your Objective

Maintain Fiduciary Guardrails

Uphold your statutory duties of care and loyalty under intense operational pressure. Guard against legal liabilities.

STAGE 1/5
Dilemma 1: The CEO's Acquisition Deal
Part 1: Conflict of Interest

The CEO's Family Acquisition

Zenith’s charismatic CEO, Marcus Vance, presents a rushed proposal to acquire Apex Robotics, a private supplier of sensors, for $85 million. As Lead Independent Director, you discover through a private channels review that Apex is owned by Marcus's immediate brother-in-law. Marcus has kept this connection hidden and pushes the Board for an immediate vote this afternoon to "beat our competitors."

Part 2: Business Judgment Rule (BJR)

The Risky Tech Shift

Following the Apex complications, Zenith is facing high volatility. Marcus proposes pivoting all development resources toward a speculative solid-state battery technology. Although you think it is highly risky, Marcus has commissioned extensive, independent, and comprehensive third-party scientific studies that validate the tech. How must you act to ensure your decisions are shielded by Delaware’s Business Judgment Rule (BJR)?

Part 3: Corporate Opportunities

The Spinoff Patent

A research university approaches Zenith with a proprietary license for solid-state material processing. This license falls perfectly inside Zenith's core line of business. During a coffee break, another board member whispers to you: "Zenith's board is too slow to adopt this. Let's form our own personal LLC, purchase the license ourselves, and lease it back to Zenith at a premium."

Part 4: Caremark Duties

The Safety Red Flag

An anonymous whistleblower from your logistics software division files a complaint alleging that a core product package contains critical sensor flaws that could cause automated systems to fail. Marcus assures you, "We've got an internal team reviewing it next quarter, don't worry about standard safety alerts." Under Delaware's Caremark guidelines, what must you do?

Part 5: Corporate Objective

The Activist Hostile Takeover

An activist investment firm, Apex Capital, acquires a 9% stake in Zenith and demands that the board cancel its long-term sustainable energy R&D program to distribute an immediate, one-time cash dividend. Delaware courts govern the core objective of public fiduciaries. How must you weigh this short-term pressure against your duties?
